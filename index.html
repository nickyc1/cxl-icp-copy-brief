CXL ICP Messaging Brief

Two ICPs. Two purchase motions. Do not collapse them.

CXL is selling into two different buying jobs: the individual marketer trying to move up and the team leader trying to increase output without headcount. Same brand. Different anxiety. Different proof.

2 tracks

Separate the buyer before writing the copy. Volume and ACV do not respond to the same message.

ICP 2 · Team Purchase Higher ACV

Heads of Marketing, Directors (Series A-C)

VP Marketing, Head of Growth, Director of Demand Gen. Manages 3 to 15 people. Resource constrained, ambitious, and under pressure to make the team more effective.

The question they're asking "Will this make my team more effective without adding headcount?"

The operating reality

  • 72% under high or extreme pipeline pressure
  • 46% cite attribution as the #1 pain
  • 32% cite team capacity as a top-three pain
  • Running enterprise programs with mid-market headcount

Budget context

84% of budgets are flat or growing. Agencies cut 30 to 50%. Money moves toward internal capability, making training the natural destination.

CXL's edge across both motions

No theory, no fluff. For ICP 1, CXL is the skill path their boss can see. For ICP 2, CXL is faster and cheaper than adding headcount or agency retainers.

How to use this.

This page should guide ad copy, landing page modules, emails, and creative briefs. It is not a new product strategy. It is a message split.

Keep separate

  • Separate campaign/ad group labels for individual vs team.
  • Separate creative hooks, not just swapped subcopy.
  • Separate landing page entry modules when possible.

Creative rule

  • ICP 1 leads with career mobility, strategic skills, promotion, and getting out of the weeds.
  • ICP 2 leads with capacity, pipeline pressure, internal capability, agency replacement, and team leverage.
  • CXL proof stays practical in both: practitioner-led workflows, not theory.
